# Research, metrics and first-client pilot

## 1. Research objective

Validate whether independent tutors experience costly fragmentation or checking work and whether the proposed closed learning loop creates meaningful value.

## 2. Research principles

- ask about recent real behavior, not hypothetical enthusiasm;
- separate tutor and learner problems;
- do not recruit minors directly;
- record evidence and contradictory findings;
- do not treat polite interest as purchase intent;
- do not claim causal learning improvement from a short pilot;
- distinguish observed metrics, self-report and hypotheses.

## 3. Tutor interview guide

Ask about the most recent real case:

1. Tell us about the last learner assignment you created.
2. Where did you send it?
3. Where did the learner answer?
4. How did you check it?
5. How long did checking and feedback take?
6. What information did you need to remember or search for?
7. How do you record recurring mistakes?
8. How do you decide what to cover next?
9. What happens when feedback is delayed?
10. How often does this workflow occur?
11. Which tools do you currently pay for?
12. What would make you distrust AI checking?
13. Which parts must remain under your control?
14. How do you currently handle learner and parent consent/data?

Avoid:

- “Would you use our product?”
- “Do you like AI?”
- “Would this be useful?”

Instead ask for behavior, cost, frequency and existing workaround.

## 4. Learner or guardian research

Subject to consent and safe recruitment:

- ask how the learner handles a mistake between lessons;
- ask which explanations help or confuse;
- observe assignment completion on a phone;
- ask whether AI feedback is distinguishable from tutor feedback;
- assess whether hints support learning or reveal answers;
- avoid collecting sensitive background information unrelated to the product.

## 5. Pilot offer

`[PROPOSED]` Offer an adult tutor a free, concierge-supported short pilot:

- team sets up the workspace;
- team imports a small set of authorized materials/tasks;
- tutor selects one to several learners subject to consent requirements;
- team supports one real assignment cycle;
- tutor provides honest post-use feedback;
- any public quote or metric requires explicit permission and anonymization.

No paid commitment is required during the hackathon pilot.

## 6. Pilot eligibility

Preferred pilot tutor:

- adult independent tutor;
- actively teaches school students;
- regularly assigns work;
- willing to use a narrow pilot flow;
- can provide lawful, non-confidential task material;
- understands AI limitations;
- agrees to feedback and safe data practices.

`[OPEN]` First subject and tutor identity.

## 7. Pilot protocol

### Before

- explain product and limitations;
- agree on scope and duration;
- document consent/permission approach;
- decide which data may be stored;
- use aliases where possible;
- establish baseline workflow/time;
- prepare support contact and incident response;
- verify deletion/cleanup procedure.

### During

- observe onboarding;
- note assistance required;
- record timestamps for major steps;
- do not alter metrics after the fact;
- log product failures and manual workarounds;
- let tutor review AI output before using it as trusted educational feedback according to the selected policy;
- avoid publishing learner-identifying screenshots.

### After

- interview tutor;
- collect usefulness and trust ratings;
- record AI edits/rejections;
- compare baseline and pilot time cautiously;
- ask whether tutor would repeat use and why;
- request separate permission for any quote;
- document negative findings;
- clean up data according to the agreed process.

## 8. Metrics framework

### Activation

- tutor completes onboarding;
- first learner relationship created;
- first assignment published;
- learner opens first assignment.

### Core completion

- learner submits assignment;
- AI analysis returns valid output;
- tutor reviews analysis;
- confirmed evidence updates progress.

### Efficiency

- tutor time to create assignment;
- tutor time to review submission;
- learner time to receive preliminary or approved feedback;
- number of tools/steps before and after.

### Quality and trust

- tutor confirm/correct/reject rate;
- usefulness rating;
- AI `cannot_assess` rate;
- unsafe or misleading output count;
- number of manual workarounds;
- qualitative trust concerns.

### Retention/intention

- second assignment created;
- tutor states specific willingness to continue;
- learner returns for subsequent work;
- tutor requests a missing feature.

Short-pilot retention is not long-term retention and must be labelled accordingly.

## 9. Evidence repository

Store research artifacts in an access-controlled location according to privacy rules.

Recommended repository content should contain only:

- interview guide;
- anonymized synthesis;
- aggregate metrics;
- approved anonymized quotes;
- pilot test report;
- no raw personal data unless specifically approved and protected.

## 10. Pilot success definition

Minimum success:

- one adult tutor completes onboarding;
- one assignment completes the full flow;
- tutor can review/correct AI;
- no critical safety/security incident;
- team learns at least one validated benefit and one limitation.

Strong success:

- tutor repeats the flow;
- measurable reduction in routine review time;
- high AI confirmation rate on the narrow task set;
- tutor explicitly agrees to continue after the hackathon;
- approved anonymized quote available.

Exact numerical thresholds remain `[OPEN]` until baseline data exists.
